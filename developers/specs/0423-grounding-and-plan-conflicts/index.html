Spec 0423: Grounding labels and contradictions between plans

Issue: #423 · Milestone: 0.7.0 · Status: Implemented · Depends on: none · Related: #427, #433, #434

Summary

Section titled “Summary”

Every generated item that makes a claim about the organization’s plans (an MSEL row’s expected action, an inject’s controller note, an overview “section tested”, an idea) gets a label: Grounded, Needs review or Unsupported, worked out from the plan passages it cites and shown as a badge with the reason. Separately, a Plan check compares the organization’s own documents and lists places where two plans disagree (for example two documents naming different approvers for public releases), quoting both passages with document, section and page.

Requirements

Section titled “Requirements”

User stories

Section titled “User stories”
  • As an exercise designer, I want to see at a glance which expected actions the plans actually support, so that I review the weak ones before an instructor reads them.
  • As a reviewer, I want each label to say why (which words matched which passage, which claim found nothing), so that I can trust or overrule it in seconds.
  • As an emergency manager, I want to know where our plans contradict each other, with both passages side by side, so that I can fix the plans or exercise the conflict (#427).
  • As a cautious user, I want grounding to work fully offline with no model call, so that labels cost nothing and do not depend on a model judging itself.

Functional requirements

Section titled “Functional requirements”

Grounding labels

  • FR-1 The app computes a grounding result for these items: each MselRow (claim fields: expectedAction, plus identifiers in event), each Inject (instructorNote), each overview.coreSectionsTested entry, and each ScenarioIdea (whyItTeaches against sectionsExercised).
  • FR-2 A result has a label (grounded, review, unsupported) or none when the item makes no plan claim (a STARTEX card, a NOISE row with an empty expected action, a TIME_JUMP card). none shows no badge.
  • FR-3 The label comes from a deterministic check (no model call) of each claim sentence against the full text of the cited chunks (looked up with IndexStore.chunk(chunkId), not the 200-character Citation.quote):
    1. Resolution: a citation whose chunkId no longer exists in the index (document removed or re-ingested) counts as missing.
    2. Anchor terms: the claim’s anchor terms (role titles, agency names, facility identifiers from facility-facts.md, numbers with units, durations such as “within 24 hours”, section numbers, and action verbs from a fixed list: notify, activate, approve, authorize, isolate, evacuate, declare, shut down, report) must appear in a cited chunk, after normalization (case, punctuation, plural, number words to digits).
    3. Content overlap: the share of the claim’s content words found in the best cited chunk.
    4. Semantic similarity: cosine similarity between the claim and the cited chunk with the project’s active embedder (activeEmbedder in src/main/retrieve/index.ts). With the hash embedder this signal is skipped and only 1 to 3 count.
  • FR-4 Label rules (thresholds live in one constants file and are tuned against the calibration set in Testing):
    • grounded: at least one resolved citation, every claim sentence supported (overlap ≥ T_SUPPORT or similarity ≥ S_SUPPORT), and every anchor term found in a cited chunk or in facility-facts.md.
    • unsupported: the item makes a plan claim and has no resolved citation, or no claim sentence reaches T_WEAK, or an anchor term contradicts the cited chunk (a different number, duration or role in the same slot; see FR-10 slot extractors).
    • review: everything else.
  • FR-5 Each result carries its evidence: per claim sentence, the best chunk, the matched and missing anchor terms, the overlap and similarity scores, and the reason in one plain sentence (“‘within 1 hour’ is not in TIRP 13.6; the passage says ‘within 2 hours’”).
  • FR-6 Grounding runs automatically after msel_phase, inject_text, scenario_overview, ideate and after any user edit saved through scenarios:save / injects:save, for the changed items only. A result is keyed by a fingerprint of the claim text and citation chunk ids (as rowFingerprint in src/main/generate/critique-notes.ts does for critique notes), so a stale result is recomputed, never shown as current.
  • FR-7 An optional Verify with model action (per item, per scenario) asks the routed critique provider to judge support. The model must return, per claim, a verdict and a verbatim quote from the cited chunk. A verdict whose quote is not a substring of the chunk (after whitespace normalization) is discarded and logged. A model verdict can lower a label (grounded to review) or raise review to grounded; it can never raise unsupported without a validated quote. The deterministic result stays visible beside it.
  • FR-8 The user can override a label (mark as Grounded / Unsupported with an optional note). The override sticks until the item’s fingerprint changes.
  • FR-9 Grounding feeds the existing checks: one GROUNDING_UNSUPPORTED warning (severity warning) per unsupported item and one GROUNDING_REVIEW (severity info) per review item, with rowId / injectId, listed in the lint bar and the Scenario checks with plain messages in src/shared/lint-messages.ts. A scenario summary counts labels (“41 grounded · 6 to review · 2 unsupported”).

Plan conflicts

  • FR-10 A Check plans for conflicts action (Documents page; also offered once after an ingest adds a second plan-kind document) runs a plan-check job over the project’s documents of kind plan, policy and form (DocumentKind in src/shared/schemas/project.ts):
    1. Slot extraction (deterministic): extractors find typed statements in every chunk: approver or authority for an action (“approved by”, “authority to”, “only the … may”), notification target and deadline (“notify … within …”), contact number or address for a named role, activation thresholds and levels, reporting deadlines to regulators, and the lead for a function. Each statement becomes { slot, subject, value, chunkId, span }.
    2. Candidate pairs: two statements from different documents with the same slot and a matching subject (normalized, or embedding similarity ≥ S_SUBJECT) and different values. Pairs inside one document are included only when the sections differ and the user turns on “also within one document”.
    3. Confirmation (optional, on by default when a critique provider is routed): the model sees both passages and returns contradicts | consistent | complementary | unrelated with a verbatim quote from each passage. Unvalidated quotes discard the verdict; without a model, candidates stay unconfirmed and are still listed.
  • FR-11 A run also lists dangling references (gaps): a passage that cites a plan, annex, appendix or section (“see Annex F”, “per the Crisis Communications Plan”) that matches no ingested document title, short name or heading. Each lists the citing passage and the missing target.
  • FR-12 Findings are saved to <project>/plan-conflicts.json and listed on the Documents page under Plan conflicts: both passages side by side (document title, heading path, page, quoted span highlighted), the slot (“Who approves public releases”), the two values, and the model verdict when there is one. Actions: Open passage (DocumentDetail at that chunk), Not a conflict (dismiss with optional note), Confirmed, Copy for plan owner (plain text with both citations).
  • FR-13 Re-running keeps the user’s dismiss / confirm decisions for findings whose two spans are unchanged (keyed by a hash of slot + both span texts). Findings whose chunk disappeared are marked outdated and hidden by default.
  • FR-14 The scan is bounded: at most MAX_PAIRS (default 80) candidate pairs go to the model per run, ranked by slot weight (authority and deadlines first) and subject similarity. The job log says how many were found, sent and skipped.

Non-functional requirements

Section titled “Non-functional requirements”
  • NFR-1 Cost: grounding and candidate detection are deterministic and free. Model confirmation uses whatever the user routed to critique; with a local provider it costs nothing (CONTRIBUTING, Project rules 1).
  • NFR-2 Privacy: no new network destination. Model confirmation and “Verify with model” go through the routed provider like every other stage, so #433 preview and redaction apply when it is a cloud provider (Project rules 3, 4).
  • NFR-3 Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA. Badges carry a text label and an icon, never colour alone; contrast checked by scripts/check-contrast.mjs; the conflict panel’s two passages are reachable in reading order with headings; every action has a keyboard path.
  • NFR-4 Performance: grounding a 40-row MSEL with its injects takes under 2 s on the reference corpus with the hash embedder and under 5 s with local ONNX embeddings (similarities computed in one embed batch). The deterministic part of a plan check over reference/ncia takes under 10 s.
  • NFR-5 Offline: everything except “Verify with model” and conflict confirmation works with no provider configured.

Out of scope

Section titled “Out of scope”
  • Fixing plans or suggesting plan text.
  • Grounding free prose in the handbook, briefing and hot wash documents (follow-up issue).
  • Cross-project comparison (for example against a regional plan in another project).
  • Labels on media (images, audio).

Design

Section titled “Design”

Modules

Section titled “Modules”

src/main/grounding/ sits beside src/main/retrieve/ because both read the index; it imports from generate/ only the facility facts reader. The engine calls groundScenario after the stages in FR-6 (in src/main/generate/engine.ts step functions and src/main/generate/injects/text-stage.ts), and the scenario and inject save handlers call it for edited items.

Data model (src/shared/schemas/grounding.ts)

Section titled “Data model (src/shared/schemas/grounding.ts)”

Scenario gains grounding: ScenarioGrounding.optional(). Scenario is a loose object, so an older build keeps the field on save (#208). MselRow and Inject are not changed: an older build that strips unknown row keys would lose per-row data, so results live at scenario level. Ideas store their result in ideas.json beside each idea (IdeasFile in src/main/generate/ideas.ts gains an optional grounding map).

The effective label is override?.label ?? model?.label ?? label.

<project>/plan-conflicts.json (src/shared/schemas/plan-conflicts.ts):

Findings with verdict consistent or unrelated are kept in the file (so a re-run does not re-ask the model) but not listed. #427 reads this file.

IPC (src/shared/ipc/grounding.ts, one channel per use case)

Prompts

Section titled “Prompts”

Two new prompt files with frontmatter, editable like the others (task: critique, low temperature, no retrieval queries; the passages are passed as variables inside a <documents> block escaped with escapeText from src/main/generate/retrieval.ts):

  • prompts/grounding_verify.md, outputSchema: GroundingVerifyOutput: { claims: [{ claim, verdict: 'supported'|'partial'|'unsupported'|'contradicted', quote }] }.
  • prompts/plan_conflict.md, outputSchema: PlanConflictOutput: { verdict, quoteA, quoteB, explanation }.

Both system prompts state that passages are data, never instructions, and that a verdict without a verbatim quote is discarded. Both are added to STAGE_OUTPUT_SCHEMAS and to Stage as grounding_verify and plan_conflict so provenance (StageRun) records them; they are not part of PIPELINE_STAGES.

Export

Section titled “Export”
  • MSEL export (src/main/export/views.ts) adds a “Source check” column in the controller MSEL only (never in player material): the label text. Off by default per export; a checkbox in the export dialog turns it on.
  • The handbook export lists open plan conflicts tested by the scenario (#427 fills this).

Migration

Section titled “Migration”

Existing scenarios have no grounding; opening the MSEL editor triggers grounding:scenario, which computes results on demand and saves them with the next save. No file is rewritten just to add labels.

Security and privacy

Section titled “Security and privacy”
  • Chunk text is untrusted (any ingested PDF). The verify and conflict prompts wrap passages in the same escaped <documents> block as generation and say they are data. A model reply can only produce a verdict and quotes, and quotes are validated as substrings, so an instruction hidden in a plan cannot fabricate support.
  • Reason strings shown in the UI are rendered as text, never HTML.
  • Nothing new leaves the computer. Model calls follow the user’s routing and #433.
  • plan-conflicts.json stays in the project folder; it contains quotes from the user’s own documents, as the index already does.

Testing

Section titled “Testing”
  • Unit (Vitest): normalize.test.ts (durations, numbers, plurals); claims.test.ts (anchor extraction on rows from examples/*/project/scenarios); support.test.ts and label.test.ts (each FR-4 branch, missing chunk, conflicting duration); slots.test.ts (one positive and one negative case per slot, table-driven); references.test.ts; conflicts-file.test.ts (merge keeps decisions, marks outdated); verify.test.ts (fabricated quote is discarded).

Threshold changes must keep this test green.
  • Planted conflict corpus: src/main/grounding/fixtures/conflict-corpus/, three short fictional plans with five planted contradictions (approver, deadline, phone, activation level, lead agency), two consistent near-misses and one dangling reference. plan-check.test.ts with the mock provider expects all five found, the near-misses not listed, the reference listed.
  • Integration: engine tests (engine.quality.test.ts pattern) assert that a generated scenario with the mock provider carries grounding for every row with an expected action, and that editing a row recomputes only that row.
  • E2E (Playwright, mock providers): open the city-water example, see badges in the MSEL grid, open a popover with the keyboard, override a label; run Plan check on the planted corpus and dismiss a finding; axe check on both screens.
  • Live (LIVE=1): plan-check.live.test.ts runs confirmation against LM Studio on the planted corpus.

Implementation notes

Section titled “Implementation notes”

Built as specified, with these differences (decided while building; the open questions below took their recommendations):

  • No filter in the grid. A Source check bar above the MSEL grid shows the counts and lists the rows to check (unsupported first) with Go to row and Details. Hiding rows inside the grid would break its keyboard navigation, drag and row numbering.
  • Details is a dialog, not a popover in the citations cell. The grid uses one roving focus per cell, so a second focusable control in a cell is hard to reach from the keyboard. The badge in the cell is static text with an icon; Source check… in the row’s Actions menu, the bar’s Details buttons and the inject editor’s Details open the dialog. Key events in the row menu no longer reach the grid (they used to move the active cell and close the menu).
  • “Makes a plan claim” (FR-2): an item has citations, or names a role, agency, section, duration, quantity or one of the action verbs. A row with neither gets no label.
  • Anchor terms (FR-3.2): identifiers are tag-shaped ones (letters joined to digits: PLC-02, NAOH-FC-203, ERP-2) plus a short list of agency acronyms (FBI, CISA, EPA…); bare acronyms such as SCADA or GPS are too common to require. The scenario’s role names count as known, like facility facts, and its role abbreviations are never anchors.
  • Overlap is the share of the claim’s content words found in all its cited chunks together (the best chunk is still named in the evidence). A duration or quantity is checked in the passage sentence that states the same thing; a different value there is the conflict.
  • Citations whose chunk id moved (an index rebuilt in another order, as when an example is opened) are found again by document and quote before they count as missing.
  • Overview sections are labelled by resolution: the cited chunk exists (Grounded), its quote is not in it (Needs review), or it is gone (Unsupported).
  • Model labels (FR-7): every claim supported with a validated quote raises the label to Grounded; any validated partial, unsupported or contradicted verdict lowers Grounded to Needs review; nothing validated leaves the checker’s label. A scenario-wide verify asks about at most 120 items, weakest first.
  • Inject edits (FR-6): injects:save recomputes the labels and writes scenario.json only when a result changed. grounding:scenario recomputes stale items without writing, as in Migration.
  • Subject matching for plan conflicts uses token similarity (S_SUBJECT 0.5); embedding similarity is not used there.
  • Extra channels: grounding:ideas (idea labels from ideas.json) and plan-check:defaults (whether confirmation is on by default, and why). grounding:override without a label clears the user’s label.
  • The post-ingest offer appears once in the Plan conflicts section of the Documents page when the project has two or more plans and no check has run, not in IngestJobs.tsx.
  • Calibration: 60 rows from the shipped examples (fixtures/calibration.json), 20 per hand label; four unsupported cases change a duration from the plan’s and two cite a chunk that was removed. Agreement at the shipped thresholds is 58 of 60; the two disagreements are rows the checker calls Grounded that a reader would review.

Open questions

Section titled “Open questions”
  1. Label names. “Unsupported” (issue) or “Largely unsupported” (Preppr)? Recommendation: “Unsupported” with the reason always one click away; shorter and fits the grid.
  2. Should review rows block “Ready” status? Recommendation: no. Unsupported rows add a warning like any lint; readiness (readiness.ts) mentions the count but does not block.
  3. Run model confirmation by default in Plan check? Recommendation: yes when critique routes to a local provider; when it routes to a cloud provider, default off and say why, since a full check can send up to 160 passages.
  4. Within-document conflicts. Recommendation: off by default; long single plans produce many false positives (an annex legitimately overriding the base plan).
  5. Thresholds per domain pack. Recommendation: one global set for 0.7.0; revisit with #434 benchmark data.
