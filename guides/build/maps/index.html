Maps

Maps show where injects happen. Open Maps under Advanced in the sidebar.

Site plans

Section titled “Site plans”

Add site plan takes a PNG, JPEG or WebP image up to 50 megapixels, or one page of a PDF (set PDF page first; the page is drawn at 200 dpi and the PDF is kept beside it). Add places (a name and a kind), pick one, then click the plan to pin it, or type the position as percent across and down. A place can name a facility fact such as gates:A1, and Pin facility facts lists facts not placed yet. Places belong to the project, so every scenario shares them. Give an MSEL row up to five places from its row menu, Places…. When you generate an MSEL the model is given your place names; a row whose text names a place or its fact entry exactly gets that place without any model call.

Show map on screen (Facilitate, Map tab) opens the map in the presentation window with only the delivered pins. The MSEL and controller handbook PDFs add Locations pages: each site plan with numbered markers (12 per page) and a legend of the rows.

Facilitate

Section titled “Facilitate”

The Map tab beside the timeline draws delivered rows (filled), due rows (filled with a ring and “due”) and, only when Show upcoming is on, upcoming rows (dashed). Several rows at one place show a number. Selecting a pin selects the row, and the Locations list under the map gives the same information as text for keyboard and screen reader use.

Geographic maps and map data

Section titled “Geographic maps and map data”

Add map makes a map pinned by latitude and longitude. Its map data is one of:

  • Online (Settings › Maps, off by default). When on, the map provider receives your computer’s IP address and the map tiles for the area you view. Nothing else from your project is sent. OpenFreeMap is the default. OpenStreetMap standard tiles are for viewing only, under the OSM tile usage policy: tiles are cached as their headers say (at least 7 days), never prefetched, and never saved for offline use. A custom address needs an attribution.
  • State map files, downloaded from the Action Stations maps catalog when you ask.
  • An area around a site, downloaded from OpenFreeMap within its offline terms (3,000 tiles, 50 MB, 4 requests at a time). The estimate is shown before you start.
  • Your own .pmtiles or .mbtiles file (up to 4 GB with bounds and an attribution).

Every download is started by you, checked against its checksum, and can be cancelled in the jobs tray. Files are stored once per computer under the app’s data folder and shared by projects.

Overlays

Section titled “Overlays”

A geographic map can carry overlays: the information that sits on top of the map during an exercise. An overlay layer is one of:

  • Areas, such as warning areas, watch boxes, outlook areas and flood extents.
  • Lines and tracks, such as a storm track, a road closure or a river reach. A track shows a dot at each point along it.
  • Cones and swaths, such as a forecast cone with its centre line.
  • Points, such as storm reports, gauges and assets, with a reading when there is one.
  • Grids, such as radar or a model field, drawn with a colour scale. A grid is placed by the longitude and latitude of its edges.

Below the map you get:

  • Map layers: a checkbox for each layer. Tab moves between layers and Space turns one on or off.
  • A time slider, when any overlay has a time. Each feature can have a start and an end; the map shows what is in force at the time you choose. The Earlier and Later buttons and the arrow keys step through the times, and the time is written out beside the slider (always in UTC). Show all times draws every overlay at once. A grid with several frames shows the frame in force.
  • An overlay legend: one entry for each layer. Every item has a name, and the swatches differ by line pattern and fill hatch as well as colour, so the map still reads in grey or with colour blindness. A grid’s legend lists the values of its colour scale with their unit.
  • Overlay data: the same information as a table (layer, item, kind, time, where or value), for anyone who cannot see the map.

The outline of a place (an area drawn for a place on a map) is drawn too: on a geographic map it is an overlay layer called Place areas, and on a site plan it is a dashed outline over the picture.

Overlays come from data in the project, such as the case data of a toolkit, which turns its GeoJSON datasets and grids into layers. Features that cannot be drawn (no shape, or coordinates off the globe) are left out, and the rest are drawn.

Overlays in pictures and exports

Section titled “Overlays in pictures and exports”

Show map on screen shows the overlays with the same controls. The snapshot of a geographic map is a picture of the overlays at the time you choose (or all of them) on a plain frame with the numbered places and the legend. The MSEL and handbook Locations pages draw the overlays in the same way, with the numbered places inside the drawing, the legend, a one-line summary and the data table. A page shows every overlay at once; each feature’s times are in the table. The pictures have no street map.

Attribution and licence

Section titled “Attribution and licence”

Attribution is always shown on the map. Map data from OpenStreetMap is licensed ODbL: a project that carries copied map data is distributing a Produced Work and must keep its attribution.

Map on devices

Section titled “Map on devices”

During a live session, tick Map on devices on the Facilitate Map tab. Each phone gets the map with only the places of messages that reached its seat, sent to that device alone; places appear as you send messages and disappear if you withdraw one. A site plan arrives as a sealed picture with numbered places. A geographic map shows the places as a schematic with coordinates: the phone page has no street map and fetches no tiles. Untick the box to take the map off the phones. See Live play.

Place names on offline maps

Section titled “Place names on offline maps”

Offline vector maps name places, roads and water in Latin script. The font is Noto Sans (Latin ranges only), bundled in the app under the SIL Open Font License; the licence text is included with the app. Names in other scripts are not shown.

Locations pages for geographic maps

Section titled “Locations pages for geographic maps”

The MSEL and handbook exports add a Locations page for each geographic map the MSEL uses. Places are numbered and plotted by latitude and longitude on a plain frame, with the coordinates in the legend. The page has no street map; print the map from the Maps page if you need one. A map with overlays also gets a page when no place is used (see Overlays above).

Map updates

Section titled “Map updates”

Settings, Maps has a switch to check for new state map files when the app checks for new versions. It needs the version check on, runs at most once a day, compares checksums only and downloads nothing.
